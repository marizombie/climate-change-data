Where climate monitoring stops watching

We are very good at measuring the weather and very bad at checking whether anyone was helped. This dataset shows where the gap sits: 46 monitoring systems scored on whether they trigger any action, 76 years of heat exposure for 12 southern European cities, and 12 costed fixes ranked by how hard they are to start. Every table is free to search, download and reuse.

What the data shows

Four findings, each backed by tables you can open and check yourself.

The blind spot sits where people could still be helped

Scoring 15 stages from emission to health outcome, four come out effectively unmeasured: whether a detected leak was fixed, indoor heat, what people did after a warning, and whether adaptation spending worked.

Open the gap matrix

Sensing is plentiful. Acting on it is rare.

Of 46 operational systems, only 12 are wired to anything that changes a physical outcome. Thirty are connected to nothing. Among the 18 satellite greenhouse-gas and urban-sensing systems, 17 trigger no action at all.

Open the inventory

The extremes moved far faster than the averages

From the 1960s to the 2020s, nights above 20 °C rose 1.5× across the twelve cities. Days above 35 °C rose 2.7×. Nights above 25 °C rose 6.5×. The deeper into the tail, the steeper the growth.

Most fixes are cheap because the sensing is already paid for

Nine of the twelve proposed actions cost under €1 per person per year, against about €120 for air-conditioner subsidies. Ranked by what blocks a start rather than by cost, two can start now with no permission and no hardware.

Use the data

Before you build on it

  • ERA5 is modelled, not observed. It resolves a city on a ~30 km grid. It cannot see a street or a bedroom.
  • Thresholds are conventions. 20 °C nights and 35 °C days are standard European definitions, not physiological constants.
  • Costs are engineering estimates. Use them to separate options that differ 100-fold, not by 30%.
  • The inventory is a considered sample. Actuator coupling is a judgement applied consistently, not a measurement.

The full list, and what would prove the central claim wrong, is in section 6 of the report.

We maintain this dataset

This is a living dataset, not a one-off upload. We keep the series current, fix errors when they are reported, and version every change so results built on an older release stay reproducible.

What gets updated, and when

TablesUpdate
Heat indicators, trends, decadal and recent changeExtended once a year, after ERA5 closes the previous year. 2026 values are next.
Monitoring inventory and gap matrixCorrected whenever a sourced issue is filed. Full review every six months.
Solutions, ease and costsRe-scored when a deployment reports real results, including failures.
Literature counts and corpusRun to 2025. The next refresh adds 2026.

Releases follow semantic versioning. A patch fixes values, a minor release adds rows, years or tables, and only a major release renames or removes a column. Every past release stays downloadable from the repository's tags.

Found an error? Tell us.

Corrections are the most useful thing you can send. Open an issue with a source and we will review it, credit you in the changelog, and ship the fix in the next release.

  • A mis-scored system. The actuator-coupling column drives the central finding and deserves scrutiny.
  • Real deployment experience. If a data-protection agreement took you three weeks rather than thirty, the ease scores should say so.
  • A city to add. The method is not region-specific, and the pipeline takes one line per city.
